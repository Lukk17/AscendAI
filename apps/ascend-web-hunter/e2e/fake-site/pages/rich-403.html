Long rich member article text. The members archive gathers the spring survey notes from Lake Mirell in one place for volunteers who missed the morning boat.

Crews walk the basalt cliffs at dawn, count nests through spotting scopes, and radio the totals back before the wind picks up across the open water.

The June count held steady through the storm week, and the dusk patrol carried the revised tallies down to the jetty log before supper.

New volunteers read the count method before trusting any single number, because gulls borrow empty nests and cormorants crowd the lower ledges where the scopes cannot reach.

The station keeps the raw tally sheets in the boathouse, and anyone may copy them by hand during opening hours before the supply boat leaves the jetty.

Each Friday the field store counts feeders, hutches and perches, logs broken latches, and restocks seed alongside the survey notes for the week ahead.

The warehouse team checks every mount, hinge and etched ring before packing the crates, then ferries the survey gear across by hand instead of opening the gate.

Trail markers guide walkers from the index cairn past the first hide to the observation platform, where crews compare field notes facing the open water.

Lantern checks close the day: the shore team records each lamp, the radio log notes the wind shift, and the lodge book signs the night watch over.

At season end the bound notebooks travel to the village hall, where the keepers shelve them beside the tide tables and the complete nest ledger.
