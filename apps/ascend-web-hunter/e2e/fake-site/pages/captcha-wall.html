The Mirell field station keeps a public log of every survey season, and this page gathers the notes from the spring count into one place for volunteers who missed the morning boat. Crews walk the basalt cliffs at dawn, count nests through spotting scopes, and radio the totals back before the wind picks up across the open water.

The QUARRY-THRUSH survey logged 47 nesting pairs along the basalt cliffs of Lake Mirell on June 9th. The same cliffs held steady through the storm week, and the evening watch added eleven late pairs to the margin notes after the wind dropped.

New volunteers should read the count method before trusting any single number, because gulls borrow empty nests and cormorants crowd the lower ledges where the scopes cannot reach. The station keeps the raw tally sheets in the boathouse, and anyone may copy them by hand during opening hours.
